Preview overlays: camera control and time control

An interactive mockup inside a stand-in for the app window. The globe is a quick WebGL approximation that follows the app's camera model and puts the terminator where the Sun really is. Every overlay control works, the mouse gestures on the globe work as in the app, and the sidebar sliders stay in sync. Press F1 for the shortcut sheet (⌘? in macOS mode).

Mouse and keyboard

Camera panel

  • Always expanded. While the pointer is elsewhere the background disappears and the controls drop to about a third.
  • Hovering or using the panel brings it back. A gesture on the globe lights up only the matching control, at full strength, even while the panel is faded.
  • Orbit and Frame are joysticks that move the camera the way you push, so the globe slides the other way. The orbit arrows step 10°.
  • Zoom carries a mark at its default and snaps to it when the thumb comes close.
  • Drag the ring to roll, click N for north up. ↺ appears only when a value differs from its default.
  • No lens control: the field of view stays in the sidebar's Framing group.

Time bar

  • The pill opens into the bar on hover. The pin in the corner keeps the bar open and at full strength wherever the pointer goes.
  • Play/pause sits centered over the tracks, with a speed ruler on each side: backward on the left, forward on the right, the slowest speed next to the button. Click a dot or drag along a ruler to run at that speed. J K L walk along the rulers, R jumps to the same speed on the other side.
  • Play runs one of the two tracks, chosen with the button in front of its label. Time: the clock runs, 1× to 6 h/s, and the date follows. Date: the date runs and the time of day stays, 1 day/s to 3 months/s, so the terminator swings through the seasons. The rulers show the speeds of the track in use.
  • The speed readout sits left of the controls; click it for every speed in both directions.
  • The clock block sits beside the three rows: Live / Now with the offset, the time with its zone right after it, and the date.
  • Times are local by default; the zone chip switches to UTC and back, and the time track follows.
  • The time track is shaded with day, twilight and night under the camera. Diamonds on the date track are the equinoxes and solstices; the handle snaps to them.
  • Click the time or the date for exact entry, the zone to switch between UTC and local time.

Not the real renderer: no sky lens, no Moon, no seasonal textures, and a static cloud map. In macOS mode the page accepts Ctrl as well as ⌘, because Windows and Linux desktops keep the ⌘ (Windows) key for themselves. The Sun position is a low precision almanac formula, good to a fraction of a degree.
