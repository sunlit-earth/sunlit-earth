# Plan: Celestial D, the Milky Way

Phase D of [2026-08-23-celestial-bodies-research.md](2026-08-23-celestial-bodies-research.md), section 8. Builds on phase A's `SkyState` and star sprites ([2026-08-23-celestial-a-stars-plan.md](2026-08-23-celestial-a-stars-plan.md)), on phase B's inverse of the sky lens, `sky_lens_direction` in `sphere.wgsl` ([2026-08-23-celestial-b-sun-plan.md](2026-08-23-celestial-b-sun-plan.md)), and on phase C's slot layout ([2026-08-23-celestial-c-moon-plan.md](2026-08-23-celestial-c-moon-plan.md)).

## Summary

The diffuse Milky Way as a background layer: NASA SVS Deep Star Maps 2020's Milky-Way-only layer (faint Gaia starlight with the bright stars excluded, so nothing is drawn twice above phase A's sprites), equatorial J2000 variant, tone-mapped offline from the 4k EXR to an 8-bit JXL, rendered by a fullscreen draw that reconstructs the view ray per pixel through the inversion the Sun's glare already uses and samples the panorama through the transposed sky rotation. One intensity parameter. The layer follows the texture resolution cap, adds a resolution-dependent budget term, and the pass's clear color moves to near black.

## Stakes Classification

Low-medium. Additive rendering plus one LFS asset plus a budget bump. The failure modes are visual (a misrotated band, a mip seam at the panorama wrap, a tone-map that reads wrong) and one memory-accounting update; nothing destructive, nothing persisted beyond a config field. The clear-color change touches every existing golden, which makes it the one step with repo-wide blast radius, handled as its own step with a full regeneration.

## Research

Recorded from code reconnaissance on 2026-08-23, references to the tree at that date.

Golden tolerance and the pinned datetime are per `tests/golden.rs:109-180`.

## Key Design Decisions

1. **A fullscreen draw, first in the pass, no depth interaction, through the inversion that already exists.** *(Revised after phase B; see Revisions after phase B, 1.)* The fragment shader calls `sky_lens_direction` on its framebuffer position, which undoes the pan and inverts the sky lens exactly as the Sun's glare does, then rotates by the transposed view rotation and by the transposed `R_world_from_eqj` into EQJ, and converts the direction to equirectangular UV. No second inversion is written: the one in the tree has its pan sign pinned and shares its edge-radius clamp with the forward map, and a copy would be a rule spelled twice, which phase B's departure 7 is about. The vertex side is the four-vertex quad the sun draws generate from `vertex_index`, through the same pipeline template with `buffers: &[]`, rather than an oversized triangle; the two cover the same pixels and one of them exists. This is exact at every zoom and immune to the far plane, which is why it wins over an inside-out sphere whose radius would have to thread between the maximum camera distance and the far plane forever (research document, section 8.2). Stars draw second and sit on top; the Sun's disk, the Moon, the Earth and everything after overdraw it by order, and the Sun's glare, drawn last, veils it as it veils everything under it.

   The inversion is total, which is what makes a fullscreen primitive exactly right rather than one that needs a guard. The lens normalizes the horizontal half field of view to the screen edge, so a pixel at NDC (x, y) inverts through `theta = 2 * atan(length(vec2(x, y / aspect)) * edge_radius)`, and the largest value that can produce is at the screen corner: 77 degrees at the default 140 degree sky on a 16:9 frame, 98 at the widest setting of 180. Both are short of the 180 degrees where the projection diverges, so every pixel of the frame has a direction and there is no region outside the field of view to leave undrawn. The shader's `sky_corner_angle` is that number computed per frame, pan included, for the sun draws' culls.

2. **The wrap seam is handled with explicit gradients from the start.** The atan2-derived U coordinate jumps a full texture width at the wrap column, which makes hardware mip selection pick the smallest level for one pixel column; the shader computes UV gradients from the direction's derivatives (or equivalently samples with `textureSampleGrad` using gradients built from a continuous auxiliary coordinate) rather than shipping the artifact and patching it later. *(Revised after phase B; see Revisions after phase B, 2.)* The fix is pinned by an engine case with a per-column assertion, not by a golden. A wrap column is one pixel wide: in a 512 by 256 frame that is 256 pixels, 0.2 percent of the frame, under the one percent of outliers a golden tolerates and nothing at all in its mean, so a seam golden would pass with the seam in it, which is the failure phase B's departure 2 measured for a Sun at the default sky. The case renders the fixture panorama with the wrap in frame and asserts that no column differs from both its neighbors by more than a step, with the fixture's content smooth across the wrap so a mip drop shows as a distinct band; it has to fail when the gradient sample is replaced by a plain `textureSample`, which is the fault it exists to catch. A golden may still be framed across the seam for the picture, but it is not the test.

3. **The asset is the equatorial 4k `milkyway_2020` layer, tone-mapped offline, and the exposure is decided once with screenshots.** Maintainer prep: download the 4096x2048 EXR, tone-map the linear half-float data to 8-bit (the exposure and curve chosen against night-side screenshots during Step 1, then recorded in the provenance note), orient to the loader's convention, encode with cjxl, commit to `textures/` under LFS with provenance and the credit line, which also joins the About window's attribution list. The in-app intensity slider covers taste at runtime; the baked exposure only has to be a sensible neutral.

   What Step 1's screenshots also have to settle is whether 4096 is enough resolution, which phase A's field-of-view control turned into a question. The panorama carries 11.4 texels per degree; a 4K render shows 23.9 screen pixels per degree at the default 140 degree sky and 62.5 at the narrowest 60, so the layer is magnified between two and five and a half times depending on where the user leaves the slider. A diffuse band is the kind of content that tolerates magnification, which is why this plan still specifies 4096, but it is a judgment to make with a night-side screenshot at both ends of the slider and not an assumption to ship. The 8192 variant would match the default exactly and quadruple the budget term; if it is taken, record it as a departure and re-measure decision 7.

4. **The layer is a file-backed slot under the resolution cap.** Next slot after the moon; loads async through the mailbox like every other slot; excluded from `textures_ready`/`textures_pending` (an overlay, like clouds and the moon); absent without the file. At the 8192 and 4096 settings the 4096 source loads as is; at 2048 the halving cache serves the downscale (10.7 MiB resident instead of 42.7 MiB). A resolution switch purges and reloads it with the other file-backed slots, and the cache makes the reload cheap.

5. **One scene parameter, `milky_way_intensity`, defaulting on at a modest value on every tier.** The layer reads phase A's `sky_fov` through the same inversion the stars use, so it rescales with them when that slider moves and needs no field of view of its own; a test that the band's screen extent tracks `sky_fov` while the Earth's does not is what keeps the two lenses from drifting apart. Full digest treatment, Celestial group slider, 0 skips the draw through a `MilkyWay::select` in the shape of `Stars::select` and `Sun::select`. The research document's open question about the low tier resolves here: the memory cost follows the resolution setting rather than the tier (the tier caps MSAA and preview width, not textures), so there is no tier-shaped reason to default it off; if the soak or budget numbers disagree during Step 4, that is a departure to record.

6. **The clear color moves to near black as its own step, with a full golden regeneration.** With a real sky behind everything, the blue-tinted clear reads as haze under the panorama and, where the panorama is dark, as a floor the stars sit on. Move it to (0.005, 0.005, 0.01) unconditionally (not conditional on the layer, so frames without the asset do not change color when it arrives), regenerate all goldens on the local adapters in one commit that contains nothing else, and dispatch `golden.yml` for the Metal set. Kept last so every other step validates against unmoved references. The move takes the three engine cases that hardcode the clear color with it (`tests/engine.rs`), and the set it regenerates is the nine cases the suite holds after phase B plus whatever phase C added. The `metal` set is already behind by phases A and B and no machine here can regenerate it, so the dispatch is for the whole backlog and not for this step alone.

7. **The budget term is resolution-dependent.** `resident_texture_bytes` gains the panorama at the width the cap yields; tests re-measured and re-pinned per the phase 4.1 convention, including the both-directions property (the budget clears a cold run at every resolution and stays under twice one).

## Risks and Mitigations

- The tone-map is a one-shot artistic decision baked into the asset. Mitigated by deciding it with screenshots before the LFS commit and by the runtime slider; a re-bake is a one-file LFS replacement if taste moves later.
- Ray reconstruction has two transposes to get right after the inversion, and each wrong sign yields a plausible-looking but misrotated sky. The inversion itself is phase B's and is pinned; the Sagittarius check catches gross errors in what this phase adds after it. The round-trip guard is not a CPU re-derivation of the lens, which would be a third spelling of the chain to keep in step: it is a compute probe in the shape of `the_shader_and_the_cpu_agree_on_the_two_shared_rules`, feeding directions through the production forward projection (the function phase C factors out of `vs_star`, or factored here if C has not) and back through `sky_lens_direction`, asserting they return, over the field of view's whole range and both signs of pan. Phase B's pan case can also be run with the fixture panorama on, since the band moving with the stars under a pan is the same property for one more consumer.
- The clear-color regeneration invalidates every reference at once; done as an isolated commit with nothing else in it, disagreement between adapters surfaces as exactly one reviewable diff set, and `golden.yml` covers Metal once this is on the default branch.
- The seam fix depends on derivative behavior that differs subtly across adapters; the seam case runs on every adapter the suite runs on rather than assuming uniformity.
- Fixture panoramas keep the engine tests independent of the LFS asset, following the existing skip-with-reason convention for the cases that truly need the real asset.

## Revisions after phase A

Phase A landed with a design that moved during implementation: celestial content is drawn through a stereographic sky lens with its own field of view (60 to 180 degrees, default 140) rather than through the Earth camera's 20 degree perspective lens. That is recorded in phase A's own Departures. This plan was written against the perspective lens and had its ray-reconstruction sentence updated when the change landed; read against the shipped tree on 2026-08-24, three further things follow.

1. **The inversion is total, so the fullscreen triangle needs no guard.** Every pixel of the frame, corners included, maps back to a direction short of the antipode: 77 degrees off the view axis at the screen corner at the default sky, 98 at the widest. Decision 1 now records the formula and the two numbers, because "invert the projection" over a lens with a bounded field of view invites a guard for pixels outside it, and there are none.

2. **The layer's on-screen scale is a user control now.** `sky_fov` magnifies the panorama along with the stars, which is right and is the point, but it means the resolution question has a range rather than an answer: the 4096 source is magnified about two times at the default and five and a half at the narrowest setting on a 4K render. Decision 3 keeps 4096 and makes the check explicit in Step 1 instead of assuming it; decision 5 adds the test that the band tracks `sky_fov` while the Earth does not.

3. **The forward map is now something to be the exact inverse of.** Phase A's `vs_star` clamps the field of view, divides by `edge_radius`, and applies an aspect factor and the screen offset in a particular order. A reconstruction that differs anywhere in that chain puts the band at a slightly different scale from the stars on top of it, which reads as a misalignment rather than as a bug. The risks section asks for a round-trip test rather than a re-derivation.

Unchanged by any of this: the asset choice and its offline tone-map, the seam handling, the slot and cap behavior, the budget term, and the clear-color step, which is still last and still its own commit.

## Departures

Recorded during implementation on 2026-08-27, in the order the plan met reality.

1. **The asset is encoded by ImageMagick's libjxl rather than by cjxl, and losslessly.** This machine has libjxl 0.11.1 through ImageMagick and no `cjxl` binary, and the plan named the tool rather than the format; phase C's departure 2 is the same departure for the same reason. What the two commands do is in `textures/PROVENANCE.md`: `magick milkyway_2020_4k.exr -set colorspace RGB -colorspace sRGB -depth 8` declares the EXR's data linear without converting it and then applies the sRGB transfer curve, and `magick milkyway_srgb.png -quality 100` encodes it. Quality 100 is lossless, at 9.9 MB. The lossy alternatives were measured through the app's own `jxl-oxide` path against the PNG: quality 99 is a mean channel difference of 0.92 of 255 with a worst pixel off by 27 at 4.6 MB, 97 is 1.43 and 36 at 3.3 MB, 95 is 1.69 and 36 at 2.7 MB, 90 is 2.91 and 42 at 1.6 MB. Lossless was taken for two reasons: this asset is Gaia photon noise over a smooth gradient, which is the content a lossy codec handles worst, and it is the one texture that covers the whole frame, so an artifact in it is a property of every pixel of the sky rather than of a thirty-pixel disc.

2. **The tone map is the sRGB transfer curve and nothing else, and the exposure decision moved to the runtime default.** The plan asked for "the exposure and curve chosen against night-side screenshots during Step 1, then recorded in the provenance note". What the screenshots settled instead is that the asset wants no gain at all: the renderer works in display values throughout, since the surface textures, the clear color and every additive sky draw are in that space, so the honest baked value for a linear source is the plain transfer curve. Nothing clips, because the source's own maximum is 1.0 and about sixteen pixels reach it. The taste decision then lives entirely in `milky_way_intensity`, whose default of 0.5 was chosen from the renders in the run directory: at 0.3 the band is easy to miss behind the globe, at 0.8 and above it is the subject of the picture rather than its background, and at 0.5 it reads as a sky the Earth is in. The sRGB curve's linear toe rather than a plain gamma is what keeps the faint sky a few code values off black instead of on it, which matters because the layer is then multiplied by a number below one.

3. **The direction-to-texel map is written against what the loader produces, and the loader's own transformations are undone in the shader rather than baked out of the asset.** `assets::texture_loader::orient` mirrors every equirectangular source it loads and shifts it a quarter width, for the sphere mesh's sake, and it runs on this asset as it runs on the Earth's and the Moon's. The panorama is not sampled by mesh UVs, so that orientation is not what it wants, and there were two places to put the correction: pre-shift the file offline so the loader's pass cancels, or invert the loader's pass in the shader. The shader has it, as `PANORAMA_RIGHT_ASCENSION_ZERO` and one comment. A pre-shifted file looks wrong when a person opens it and a maintainer who re-bakes it from the source without reading the provenance note gets a sky rotated ninety degrees in right ascension, which is exactly the plausible-looking misrotation the plan's risk section is about; a constant in the shader is visible to anyone reading the function.

   What that correction rests on is a measurement rather than an assumption, and the measurement is the reason this is a departure rather than a detail. The plan says "orient to the loader's convention" as if the source's own layout were known. It is not documented by the SVS, so it was measured before the asset was committed: crops of the EXR at the galactic center, the two Magellanic Clouds, Carina, Crux, Cygnus and Cassiopeia are all bright and crops at the two galactic poles are the darkest of the set, which fixes the layout as the standard astronomical all-sky one (right ascension zero at the center, increasing to the left, north up) and rules out the mirrored reading, which gets the poles and the plane backwards. `textures/PROVENANCE.md` records it. The galactic center alone cannot settle it, which is worth knowing: it sits at right ascension 266, and 270 is the fixed point of that mirror, so the bulge lands in the same place under both readings to within four degrees. The Magellanic Clouds are what separate them.

4. **4096 is enough, at both ends of the slider.** The plan asked for this to be checked rather than assumed, and the check is `sagittarius-60-i50.png` in the run directory: the galactic center at 60 degrees of sky, the layer magnified about five and a half times, which is the most this renderer can do to it. There is no pixelation and no blockiness in it. What is visible is fine grain, and that is Gaia photon noise in the source rather than a resolution limit, so the 8192 variant would carry the same grain at four times the budget term. Decision 7 therefore stands as written.

5. **The parameter plumbing landed before the pipeline, and the slot and its budget term landed with the slot rather than in Step 4.** `SceneParams` cannot gain a field without `AppConfig` gaining one, and the shader cannot read a uniform that is not encoded, so `milky_way_intensity` went through the config, the digest, the digest table, `Uniforms` and the Slint bridge in its own commit first. This is the third phase in a row to record this (phase B's departure 5, phase C's departure 1), and phase C's suggestion that these plans should stop separating the two is now three for three. The budget term moved for a different reason: memory is a property of the slot, so `resident_texture_bytes` gained the panorama in the commit that gave it a slot, and Step 4 kept the measurement it is actually for.

6. **Criterion 6's bound does not hold on the software adapter, by a factor of eight, and the whole frame's numbers are these.** Every figure below is the mean of eight or twelve `export_pixels` calls after a warm-up, with every texture already resident, on the same engine.

   | frame | layer off | layer on | the layer | the cloud shell |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | warp, 1920x1080 | 47.0 ms | 180.3 ms | 133.3 ms | 15.9 ms |
   | warp, 3840x2160 | 159.8 ms | 714.7 ms | 554.9 ms | not measured |
   | this machine's GPU, 1920x1080 | 2.6 ms | 2.8 ms | 0.2 ms | 0.0 ms |
   | this machine's GPU, 3840x2160 | 8.4 ms | 9.3 ms | 0.9 ms | not measured |

   So on warp the layer costs eight times what the draw the criterion names it against costs, and it scales with the pixel count rather than with anything about the sky: 4.2 times the pixels for 4.2 times the cost. The research document's stated bound (`strictly less work than the existing cloud shell draw`) is wrong about a CPU rasterizer, and it is right about a real one, where the whole frame including the readback is 2.8 ms.

   Where it goes is not the texture fetch, which is what the research assumed. Dropping the shared sampler's anisotropy from 16 to 1 moves the figure by 1.4 ms, from 130.3 to 131.7 in the run that measured it, which is inside the noise between runs. It is the per-pixel angular math over the whole frame: `fs_milky_way` evaluates an `atan`, a `sin`, a `cos`, an `atan2`, an `asin`, a `sqrt` and the `sin` inside `dither` for each of two million pixels, where the cloud shell covers about a quarter of the frame and evaluates none of them.

   What it did to the suite is nothing measurable in the two longest runs and about 25 seconds in the third, and the reason is which configurations have a panorama in them at all. The soak test builds `EngineConfig::headless` and never fills a texture path, so its slot stays empty, `MilkyWay::select` returns `None`, and no panorama is drawn in any of its fourteen simulated days: it takes 51.1 s on warp, which is where it was. The golden suite is 512 by 256, where the same measurement scales to about 8 ms a frame, so its two new references add something like 16 ms to a 1.7 s run and its other twelve cases have the layer switched off entirely. The engine suite went up by about 25 s to 101 s, and almost all of that is engine startup and fixture decode rather than the draw: the five fixture cases take 17.9 s between them and each starts its own device, and the resolution-cap case takes 7.5 s decoding the real 9.9 MB asset three times.

   Nothing was done about the cost in this phase, and the lever is named rather than left to be rediscovered. `sky_lens_direction` computes `theta = 2 * atan(t)` and then `sin(theta)` and `cos(theta)`, which are algebraically `2t / (1 + t^2)` and `(1 - t^2) / (1 + t^2)`: three transcendentals for two divisions, exactly, with no approximation. It would not reach the criterion's bound even so. It removes three of the seven, and the two the equirectangular map needs (`atan2` for right ascension, `asin` for declination) cannot go anywhere, so the best it can be worth is roughly halving 133 ms, which is still four to five times the cloud shell's 16. It is not taken here for a second reason as well: that function is the Sun's glare's as well as the panorama's, so substituting it moves every existing golden, and criterion 7 allows exactly one regeneration which the clear color already owns. It is on the roadmap under Rendering. What the layer keeps in the meantime is the research document's own mitigation: it is skippable by its own parameter, and the frames the app renders are a preview that redraws only on change and a wallpaper export that happens on a schedule.

7. **The seam case's first fixture had no teeth, because a linear ramp is a fixed point of the mip chain.** Decision 2 asks for a fixture "with the content smooth across the wrap so a mip drop shows as a distinct band", and the obvious such fixture is a gradient in declination, constant in right ascension. It does not work: box-averaging and bilinear interpolation both reproduce a linear ramp exactly, so every mip level of it holds the same values and a sample that landed on the wrong one read the right answer. Measured: with `textureSampleGrad` replaced by a plain `textureSample`, the frame's largest step between neighboring sky pixels went from 2 to 3, and the case passed. The fixture is bands of declination now, eight cycles over the sky, which every level above the fourth averages away; the same fault then reads 102 against a clean 4.

   Two more things about that case moved with the fixture. The metric is a second difference over the whole frame rather than the plan's per-column count, because the branch cut is a curve on screen rather than a column, and it is two pixels wide rather than one, since a derivative is a property of the fragment quad. And the case runs at the default 140 degrees of sky, where the numbers above were taken: a second difference does not need the layer magnified, because at 140 degrees a cycle of the fixture's bands still spans seventy pixels or more and its own curvature is a fraction of a code value, while the artifact is two pixels wide and carries its whole height.

8. **Two tolerances set from the warp measurement were too tight for lavapipe, and one of the two has a narrow margin there.** The round-trip probe is the easy one: the chain returns a direction to 6.5e-7 of chord distance on warp and to 1.5e-4 on lavapipe, which is half a hundredth of a degree and is the two adapters' transcendentals rather than anything about the chain, so the tolerance is 1.0e-3, an order of magnitude above the worse of them and three below the faults, which read 1.229 and 0.546 on both.

   The seam case is the awkward one. The clean frame's largest second difference is 4 on warp and 7 on lavapipe, so the six that warp's measurement suggested failed on lavapipe on a frame with nothing wrong in it. Twelve sits between the clean pair and the faulted one, which is 102 on warp and 18 on lavapipe. That the artifact is five times weaker on the second adapter is unexplained and is the reason the plan's own risk entry asks for this case on every adapter rather than one: the case does fail under the fault on both, but on lavapipe it fails by 18 against 12 rather than by 102 against 12, and a future change that halved the artifact again would go unnoticed there.

9. **The mapping test is against the star path rather than against a re-derivation, and it is the strongest thing here.** The plan's criterion 1 asks for a planetarium check followed by a golden. A golden cannot make that claim: the references cannot depend on a Git LFS asset, so what a golden shows is a fixture. So the map from a direction to a texel is pinned instead by `the_panorama_puts_a_landmark_where_the_star_path_puts_the_same_direction`: a fixture landmark painted at Sirius's own coordinates has to land on Sirius's own sprite. Sirius because it is the only catalog entry brighter than magnitude -1, so a limit there leaves one sprite in the whole sky, and both measurements are taken against the same frame with the layer or the stars switched off, so the painted globe is subtracted rather than reasoned about. The landmark's centroid comes out at (105.93, 106.48), the sprite's at (106.23, 106.81), and the CPU lens's own answer at (106.72, 107.27), all inside a pixel of each other. That holds the panorama to the path phase A checked against ephemerides, with no lens spelled out in the test.

   What it cannot catch is the reading of the asset's layout being wrong, since the fixture and the shader are written from the same reading. `the_real_panorama_has_the_galactic_plane_where_the_plane_is` is what covers that, on the real asset and skipping with a printed reason without it: the rendered sky reads a mean of 311 of 765 at the galactic center, 354 through Carina, 220 through Cygnus, and 55 and 62 at the two galactic poles. Each of the three faults that could produce a wrong reading was injected: the quarter-width shift not undone puts the poles at 172 and 287 against a center of 101, mirroring right ascension puts the center at 137 against Carina's 342, and flipping declination puts it at 131 against poles of 97 and 88. All three fail, and all three also take the landmark out of the frame entirely, so the mapping case fails too.

10. **Criterion 2 was checked against the asset rather than against a render.** The criterion asks that a framing containing Orion show the belt as sprites with no smeared duplicates beneath. Identifying Orion's belt by eye in a 1280 pixel frame at the shipped star brightness is not something worth claiming, so the property underneath it was measured directly instead: for each of eight of the brightest stars in the sky, the mean of a 3x3 texel window at its own position against a 41x41 window around it, on the tone-mapped panorama. Sirius 1.15, Canopus 1.10, Betelgeuse 1.12, Rigel 1.22, Vega 1.01, Antares 1.26, Deneb 1.10, Altair 1.12. A magnitude -1 star baked into a diffuse layer would put tens or hundreds there; 1.1 to 1.26 is bright stars sitting in bright parts of the Milky Way. So the `milkyway_2020` layer's exclusion of the Hipparcos and Tycho stars holds in the file that shipped and not only in the SVS description of it. The renders are in the run directory and `renders.md` says what is in each.

11. **Two goldens rather than the plan's band-and-Orion pair, and the layer is off in every other reference.** With a fixture in the slot there is no band and no Orion, so the pair became the same night-side camera at the two ends of the field-of-view slider, which is the property decision 5 cares about and which two references can show between them. The layer is switched off in `base_params`, unlike the Moon's fixture, which phase C left on for every case: a layer that covers the whole frame would have moved all eleven other references and buried what each of them is for under one background, and criterion 7 wants them regenerated exactly once. Teeth: rendering either framing with the layer off is a mean channel difference of 107.60 and 107.32 against a tolerance of 2.00. Criterion 7's byte-for-byte check was run on both adapters before Step 6 and came back with only the two new files in it.

12. **The clear color moved to (0.005, 0.005, 0.01) and eleven of the twelve references moved with it on each adapter.** `close_up` is the one that did not, because the globe fills that frame and none of the clear is left in it. The three engine cases that hardcode the cleared pixel went from `[5, 5, 13, 255]` to `[1, 1, 3, 255]`, which both local adapters agree on. The `metal` set is untouched, and `golden.yml` was not dispatched: hosted minutes are billed on this repository and a dispatchable workflow is only registered from the default branch, so the metal set stays behind by phases A through D and that is an open item rather than a step of this phase.

13. **`SlotLayout` gained one clause rather than a second copy of the Moon's.** `moon()` was `(self.file_backed > MOON_SLOT - 1).then_some(MOON_SLOT)`, and `milky_way()` would have been the same comparison spelled again against a different constant. Both call one `overlay(slot)` now, and `a_layout_without_an_overlay_says_so` walks every layout size and asserts no overlay's slot is ever the cloud slot. The same factoring happened in the shader, where `sun_quad_corner` became `sky_quad_corner` and `create_sun_pipeline` became `create_sky_quad_pipeline`, because a third draw uses both and neither is the Sun's any more.

14. **The desktop e2e render case reads the picture by color, and a layer that paints every pixel of the sky broke it.** `test_render_and_exit` asserts that each corner of an 800 by 800 render is mostly space, as at least half of a 16 by 16 patch below a channel sum of 40. With the panorama on and the real asset present, the top-right corner came out at zero percent, with its brightest pixel at 373. That is the case working correctly on a picture whose premise moved: its own doc comment records that it was hardened once already, from a pixel to a patch, when the stars arrived. No threshold separates a sky with a band in it from a globe, since the band reaches 373 and the lit globe is 400 to 700, so what changed is the fixture instead: `e2e_config.toml` switches the layer off, for the same reason and in the same place it already switches the clouds off. Nothing about the staging of the asset is lost by that, because `test_memory_report` runs on the app's own defaults and is where a guest is seen to have loaded the file.

15. **One unrelated comment was corrected.** `a_git_lfs_pointer_is_not_a_texture_path` in `main.rs` justified its size threshold by "every one of the three assets is over a megabyte", which the Moon's 285 KB made false before this phase; review round 2 of phase C fixed the same sentence in the xtask and missed this one. It now names the smallest of the four assets.

16. **Criterion 2 is measured by the suite now, over the catalog rather than over eight names.** Departure 10 recorded the property by hand, and validation round 1 pointed out that a hand measurement in a document is not something a re-baked asset has to keep passing. `no_bright_star_is_baked_into_the_real_panorama` is the same measurement in `tests/engine.rs`, beside the case that pins the asset's layout and skipping with the same printed reason without the Git LFS object: the mean of a 3x3 texel window at a star's own position against the mean of the 41x41 window around it, on the file as it sits on disk. What changed from the hand version is the set. Instead of eight stars typed in by name it walks every record of the embedded HYG catalog inside magnitude 1.3, which is the same prefix the star draw submits, so the stars measured are by construction the stars the sprites draw and no coordinate is written down anywhere. All eight of departure 10's figures come back identically, Antares highest at 1.26, over 21 records that run from 0.73 to 1.26.

    The bound is 2.0 and it is a mean over the core rather than the core's peak texel, both of which are measurements rather than choices. A star baked into a diffuse layer saturates the texels it covers, and a saturated core reads 2.32 against the brightest surround in the set and about 5 against a typical one, so the wrong SVS layer fails on most of the 21 at once. Lowering the bound to 1.2 fails on Antares, which is the teeth. What the window cannot see is a star confined to one texel in the brightest part of the plane, which comes to 1.26 there and stays inside the bound; the peak texel would be the sharper metric for that case and it is not available, because the map's own Gaia grain already puts single texels at 2.08 times their local mean and there is no separation left to spend. The test's doc comment carries both numbers.

17. **The render subcommand's texture wait asks about the globe's own maps, which is a defect this phase inherited and made likelier rather than caused.** `run_render` waited for `TexturesReady` whenever any texture path was present, and `Renderer::textures_ready` reports on the globe's slots alone because the overlays are excluded from it by design. Phase C's Moon made that difference real and this phase's panorama is the fourth path in the same set, so a textures directory holding only an overlay's file spent the full two-minute timeout, logged an error about a problem that does not exist, and then saved a correct frame. The guard now asks `SlotLayout::is_globe`, which lives beside the readiness rule it belongs to and is spelled through the same `globe(mode)` the renderer uses, so a fifth overlay needs no change here. Measured against a directory holding only `milkyway_2020_4k.jxl`: 0.7 seconds and one log line where it used to be two minutes and an error. What that costs is that the same degenerate directory now renders before an overlay's decode lands, which is what waiting on `TexturesReady` never promised anyway; against the full textures directory all five uploads still arrive before the export. The roadmap's `textures_ready` entry, which is where the broken-file half of this still lives, says what the local guard asks now.

## Review Rounds

Two adversarial review rounds on 2026-08-27, each by a reviewer with no shared context, each running the gates itself rather than reading them. Neither round found a major.

1. **0 majors, 5 minors** against `7fa9925..10c5dda`, the implementation. The reviewer was asked to judge two departures as well as the diff, and upheld both: departure 6 tested the research document's assumption about where the layer's cost would be and reported it wrong with the measurement that rules out the assumed cause, and departure 14's fixture change rests on three facts the reviewer checked one by one. It re-derived the equirectangular mapping algebraically against `texture_loader::orient`, sampled the tone-mapped source at nine landmarks under both readings (the Magellanic Clouds decide it, 112 and 89 against 44 and 24 mirrored), confirmed `sky_lens_direction` is the exact inverse of `sky_lens_project` and that the two transposes compose in the right order, reproduced every teeth figure (107.62 and 107.33 for the goldens, 102 for the seam fault, 1.2287 and 0.5459 for the dropped transposes), and verified the clear-color commit contains nothing but the clear color, three constants and 22 references. The minors were absences and prose: the two new references outside the distinguishability guard, which are the closest pair in the set; a departure naming a field of view the seam case does not use; an overlay-only textures directory stalling `render` for its full timeout, reachable since phase C's Moon slot and reached again by the fourth path; a paragraph in CLAUDE.md contradicting itself on the draw order; and criterion 2's no-baked-stars property measured once by hand and by nothing in the suite. 2. **0 majors, 2 minors** against `10c5dda..6126b52`, the fix round. Every fix was verified by the reviewer's own measurement, both teeth directions where two were claimed: the guard failing at mean 0.00 on an identical pair and by name on an unnamed file, the panorama-only render finishing in 0.62 s where it used to wait two minutes, the full directory still waiting for the globe maps, the criterion 2 case failing at a bound of 1.2 on Antares, and the catalog-to-texel mapping traced through the same `panorama_texel` the landmark case pins against the star path. What this phase's rounds add to the record is a pair of fixtures that could not see their faults. ## Rollback Strategy

Feature branch. The config field is serde-ignored by older binaries, the asset is inert LFS data, and the clear-color commit reverts together with its regenerated goldens as one unit.
