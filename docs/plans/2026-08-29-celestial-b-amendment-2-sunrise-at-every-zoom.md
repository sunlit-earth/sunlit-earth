# Plan Amendment 2: Sunrise at Every Zoom, and a Finer Hand on the Globe

Amends `2026-08-23-celestial-b-sun-plan.md` and its first amendment, `2026-08-29-celestial-b-amendment-sun-at-the-horizon.md`. Written 2026-08-29, after the user looked at the shipped Sun and found it good, then zoomed in to a camera distance of 3.7 Earth radii (the zoom slider's value text is the distance) and found three things. The atmosphere glows red while the Sun is still well behind the painted globe, and the glow is gone by the time the disk appears. The disk then emerges with a dark gap between it and the edge of the Earth, where the user expected it to sit against the limb, and the red glow that should be there is faint. And the globe cannot be turned finely enough with the mouse to watch the sunrise happen, although the slider's own resolution is fine as it is. A time playback that would advance the datetime and let a sunrise run on its own was asked for as well and is deferred to `docs/roadmap.md`; this amendment does not plan it.

The first two are one defect, diagnosed below from the code and the geometry and not yet reproduced by a render, which is the first step of the work. The third is not a defect but a gearing that was never asked to resolve a thing this small.

## What changes, and what does not

Unchanged: everything the first amendment decided about the light path, the flux and exposure model, refraction, the shape of the forward lobe and its subtracted floor, the twelve controls and their provisional defaults, and the sliders' resolution. A drag at ordinary speed turns the globe as it does today, and the Slint side of the drag is untouched.

Changed:

| Today | After this amendment |
|---|---|
| The forward lobe on the Rayleigh shell is Henyey-Greenstein in the true scattering angle between the fragment's real view ray and the Sun's real direction. | The lobe is measured in the composite's own geometry: the angle through the sky lens between the fragment's screen position and the Sun's image, which is how the glare already measures every term of its own. The lobe then peaks under the emerging disk at every zoom, and the space under a refracted disk is lit troposphere rather than dark. |
| One drag gain, `0.3 × distance / 8` degrees per pixel, whatever the hand is doing. | The same gain for a moving hand, and a fixed angular rate matched to the sky lens for a slow one, blended by the cursor's speed: a slow drag moves the Sun's image about half a pixel per mouse pixel at every zoom, an ordinary sweep turns the globe as today, and there is no key to know about. |
| The sunrise was verified at one distance and one field of view (zoom 0.30, 60 degrees of sky). | Verified at four distances at the shipped 140 degrees of sky, one of them the user's 3.7, and pinned by a golden and an engine case at that distance. |

## Diagnosis

### The lobe fires in the wrong frame

The composite has two lenses. The painted globe is drawn through the camera lens (`camera_fov`, 20 degrees), the Sun through the stereographic sky lens (`sky_fov`, 140 degrees), and phase B's decision 2 resolved the mismatch by measuring everything about the limb against the painted silhouette in screen space. The first amendment kept to that rule for the disk, the glare, the transmission and the exposure, and broke it in one place: `fs_rayleigh` takes `cos_scatter = -dot(uniforms.sun_dir, v)` with `v` the fragment's real view ray, so the lobe is a function of where the Sun really is, not of where its image is.

At a distance of 3.7 the true limb is `asin(1 / 3.7)`, 15.7 degrees off the view axis. The painted limb on a 16:9 frame at 140 degrees of sky sits at an NDC radius between 0.56 (the frame's half height) and 1.15 (its corner), and the sky lens puts a direction at NDC radius `r` when `tan(θ / 2) = r × tan(35°)`, so the Sun's image reaches the painted limb when the Sun is 43 to 77 degrees off the view axis. The lobe falls to half at 30 degrees. It therefore peaks when the true Sun direction lines up with the true limb, at 15.7 degrees, while the Sun's image is still deep inside the painted disc, which is the glow before the Sun; and by the time the image reaches the painted limb the scattering angle is 30 to 60 degrees and the lobe is at or below its half, which is the glow that has gone as the disk appears. The mismatch grows as the camera approaches, because the true limb angle grows with `asin(1 / d)` while the painted limb stays where the frame puts it.

The first amendment's review sequence did not show this because it ran at 60 degrees of sky and zoom 0.30 (distance 4.9): there the true limb is 11.8 degrees off axis and the painted limb corresponds to 17 to 34 degrees through a lens that magnifies 2.3 times more, so the two frames disagreed by 5 to 22 degrees and the lobe covered the gap. At the shipped 140 degrees the disagreement is 30 to 60 degrees at the user's zoom and about 15 to 45 at the config's own 4.9.

### The gap is the unlit half of the same thing

The refraction lift is 0.46 zone widths at the horizon. At distance 3.7 the painted annulus is about 14 px on a 1080p frame and is what sets the zone, so the disk appears lifted by about 6 px, two disk diameters, and squashed to a line. That ratio is the orbital one (65.5 arcminutes of lift against 32 of disk) and the first visible light comes another 2 px up, where the band's transmission first exceeds a few percent. In the photographs the space under a lifted Sun is the troposphere glowing red and orange, never dark; in the composite that glow is the lobe, and the lobe has switched off. Lit from beneath, the disk reads as sitting on the band. Whether the lift itself also reads as too large at this zoom is a question a render answers and this amendment leaves open in criterion 2, since `sun_refraction` already scales it and a cap in disk diameters is a one-line departure if the render says so.

### The drag is smooth and too fast

There is no step anywhere in the drag. The touch area's `moved` callback hands the Rust side the delta from the previous move and records the new position, `apply_globe_drag` is continuous, the engine renders once per drained batch of parameter updates, and the digest compares camera floats exactly. What the user sees is the gearing. `degrees_per_px = 0.3 × distance / 8` scales with the camera's distance so the painted surface moves at a fixed multiple of the cursor, and the Sun's image does not: the sky lens has no zoom, and near the painted limb its image moves about 16 px per degree at 140 degrees of sky. One mouse pixel is therefore 0.14 degrees at distance 3.7 and 2.2 px of the Sun's image, 0.34 degrees and 5.4 px at the config's 4.9, while the whole band is 14 px and the squashed disk is under a pixel tall. The sunrise is seven mouse pixels long. The slider is coarser still (360 degrees over about 200 px), which the user has accepted.

## Decisions (continuing the numbering)

14. **The lobe is measured through the sky lens, like every other Sun term.** `fs_rayleigh` recovers the fragment's sky-lens ray with `sky_lens_direction(position.xy)`, which `fs_sun_glare` and `fs_milky_way` already call, and takes `cos_scatter = dot(ray, normalize(uniforms.sun_view_dir))`. The `sunlit` gate on `n_dot_l` stays, since a fragment under the Sun's image is on the lit side in either frame and the gate only ever removes the far limb. `atmo_sunrise_width` keeps its number and its hint and changes its frame: it is now the sky-lens angle at which the lobe falls to half, which is what makes the band a fixed width in pixels at every zoom, the way the glare is. Its color, `limb_hue_km` at the fragment's ray height, is unchanged: the ray height is a property of the painted shell and already agrees with the painted band. The cost is one lens inversion per shell fragment, on a rim that covers a small part of any frame; it is measured on `warp` with the paired method the flare's cost was measured by only if the shell's frame time is seen to move.

15. **The drag gain follows the cursor's speed, so a slow hand is a fine one.** The globe drag keeps two gains and blends between them by how fast the cursor is moving. The coarse gain is today's `0.3 × distance / 8` degrees per pixel. The fine gain is the angle one pixel spans at the center of the sky lens, `degrees(2 × tan(sky_fov / 4) / preview_width_px)`: 0.042 degrees per pixel at 140 degrees of sky on a 1920 px preview, so a slow drag moves the Sun's image by about half a pixel per mouse pixel near the frame's center and by about one at its edge, at every zoom, because neither side of that ratio depends on the camera's distance. Speed is the cursor's, in logical pixels per second, measured on the Rust side from the delta each `moved` callback brings and the time since the previous callback (`Instant`), with the interval capped at half a second so a pause reads as a slow start rather than as a jump, and smoothed by an exponential moving average with a time constant near 60 ms, because deltas arrive as whole pixels at whatever rate the mouse reports and a raw per-event speed is noisy by a factor of two. The gain is a smoothstep between the two in the logarithm of the speed: the fine gain at or below about 60 px per second (a pixel every 16 ms, a deliberate hand), the coarse gain at or above about 600 (an ordinary sweep), continuous and monotone between. Both thresholds and the time constant are named constants in `mouse_math` and are what step 3 tunes by hand. All of it is pure: a small `DragSpeed` state with `observe(delta_px, dt) -> speed` and a `drag_gain(speed, coarse, fine)`, both unit and property tested, and the Slint side is untouched, since `moved` already delivers what the estimator needs. The rule costs path independence: a hundred pixels of slow drag turns the globe less than a hundred pixels of fast drag, so a drag out and back does not land exactly where it started, which is the trade every desktop makes for its pointer's acceleration and the one the user chose over a modifier key. Only the globe drag takes it; framing, orientation and tilt drags keep their single gains, and the slider is untouched.

16. **Time playback is a roadmap item, not part of this amendment.** Advancing the datetime at a multiple of real time is the natural way to watch a sunrise and would make every phase B effect observable without touching the camera. It is a feature of its own with a control and a schedule in the engine, so it goes to `docs/roadmap.md` under Camera and controls with the user's ask recorded, and the speed-dependent drag is the interim.

## Controls

No new field, and nothing to learn: a slow hand is a fine one. `atmo_sunrise_width` changes frame as decision 14 says; its range, default and hint stand.

## Steps

1. **Reproduce.** Render the user's configuration (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\SunlitEarth\config.toml`: 140 degrees of sky, every Sun control at its default) at distance 3.7 (`zoom = distance_to_zoom(3.7)`) on the real textures at 1920 by 1080, at three longitudes: the true Sun aligned with the true limb (the glow with no Sun), the Sun's image one zone width inside the painted limb (the glow that should be there and is not), and the disk just emerged. Keep the three as the before pictures and measure the red excess at the limb under the Sun's image in each, so criterion 1 has its numbers before the code moves.
2. **The lobe in the sky lens.** Decision 14 in `sphere.wgsl`; the engine case of criterion 1; regenerate on both adapters and record what moved.
3. **The speed-dependent drag.** Decision 15 in `mouse_math.rs` (the fine gain, the estimator, the blend, their tests and the extended proptest invariants) and `ui_callbacks.rs` (the timestamped estimator state held by the globe-drag callback); `main.slint` unchanged. Then the three framings of step 1 by hand in the app, dragging slowly through the sunrise and sweeping across the globe, and tune the two thresholds and the time constant there; the departures record the numbers tried and the ones kept.
4. **Verification at four distances.** The sequence of the first amendment's step 3 at distances 2, 3.7, 9 and 40, at 140 degrees of sky on the real textures, judged as criterion 2 says, and the golden of criterion 4.
5. **Documentation and record.** CLAUDE.md's Sun section (the lobe's frame) and its `mouse_math.rs` line (the speed-dependent gain), `docs/roadmap.md` (the time playback item, the limb item's pointer to this amendment, the fine rotation item ticked), a one-sentence pointer under the first amendment's decision 12 saying this amendment moved the lobe's frame, and the departures below.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **The glow arrives with the Sun.** An engine case at distance 3.7 and 140 degrees of sky, latitude and framing as `horizon_camera` in the golden suite but at the user's zoom, renders the three longitudes of step 1 and measures the red excess (red minus blue, summed over the limb pixels within the glare's reach of the Sun's image) in each. It asserts the excess is larger with the image one zone width inside the painted limb than with the true Sun aligned to the true limb, and larger still with the disk emerged. Today the first comparison is inverted, which is what makes the case a test of the defect and not of a constant.
2. **The sequence reads at four distances.** The first amendment's six-frame sequence at distances 2, 3.7, 9 and 40, at 140 degrees of sky on the real textures, looked at: the band peaks under the emerging disk at each; there is no dark gap between the lit band and the disk; the disk's lifted height reads as sitting on the glow rather than floating over it. Where the last judgment fails at a distance, the departure records it and the lift's unit gets a cap in disk diameters.
3. **The speed-dependent gain.** `drag_gain(speed, coarse, fine)` returns the fine gain at and below the low threshold and the coarse gain at and above the high one, and is monotone and continuous between (a property test over speeds and over the two gains). The fine gain equals the sky lens's angle per pixel at the axis to within a part in a thousand at 60, 140 and 180 degrees of sky, is independent of zoom, and never exceeds the coarse gain: at the nearest zoom, 1.5, the coarse gain is 0.056 degrees per pixel against a fine 0.042 at 140 degrees and 0.060 at 180, so where the two would cross the fine gain takes the smaller. `DragSpeed::observe` fed a constant speed converges to it within three time constants, and an interval longer than the cap reads as the cap. At a speed above the high threshold `apply_globe_drag` reproduces today's rotation exactly, which is what keeps the existing `apply_globe_drag` tests passing as they are.
4. **A golden at the user's zoom.** `sunrise_band_close` at distance 3.7 and 140 degrees of sky, with the disk one zone width inside the painted limb so the frame shows the band and no disk, compared over a strip window the way `sunrise_band` is. Teeth: deleting the lobe (`atmo_sunrise_glow` 0) exceeds the tolerance over the window; the figures go in the record, and if the case needs an amplified glow to have teeth, it says so where `sunrise_band` does.
5. **The references that move are the ones with a band near a Sun.** Predicted to move on both adapters: `sunrise_band`, `sun_rising_through_the_band`, `sun_grazing_the_limb`, `nightglow` and `sun_over_the_night_side`, since the lobe's angle changes even where the two frames nearly agreed. Predicted to move by a residual or not at all: `default`, `rayleigh` and `clouds_across_the_terminator`. Predicted byte for byte: `close_up`, `moon_crescent`, the three star cases, the two panoramas and `cloud_terminator_close_up`. The record lists what actually happened, the way the first amendment's departure 6 did when its own prediction was wrong.
6. **Gates.** `cargo fmt --check`, `cargo clippy --all-targets`, `cargo test` on Windows against `warp`, `cargo test --workspace` in WSL against `lavapipe`, goldens regenerated on both and three consecutive green runs each.
7. **Documentation** as step 5 says, and the plan's own Departures and validation record filled in, with the shipped state of `atmo_sunrise_width` noted as unchanged in number and the thresholds and time constant of decision 15 recorded as shipped.

## Risks

- **The lobe's new frame at the widest sky.** `sky_lens_direction` has an image for every framebuffer position at every field of view the slider offers, since the antipode is past the frame's corner even at 180 degrees, so there is no position the shell can be drawn at that the inversion cannot answer. No special case is expected; the first amendment's `a_pan_past_the_frame_corner_still_draws_the_sun` family is the place to look if one appears.
- **The gate in the old frame.** The `sunlit` gate is still `n_dot_l`, a true-geometry quantity. A fragment under the Sun's image at the painted limb has its normal in the Sun's azimuth and `n_dot_l = sin θ` for a Sun θ off axis, which is positive wherever the image can be at the limb, so the gate cannot remove the band under the Sun. If a render shows otherwise the gate moves to the same frame and that is a departure.
- **The thresholds are a feel, not a measurement.** 60 and 600 px per second and a 60 ms time constant are starting points from how a hand moves, not from anything measured in this app; step 3 tunes them by hand and the record keeps what was tried. Too low a high threshold makes an ordinary drag feel sluggish; too high a low one makes the fine gain unreachable.
- **Event cadence.** Whether Slint delivers one `moved` per mouse report or one per frame changes the delta and the interval together and leaves the speed alone, which is why the estimator takes speed and not delta; the smoothing exists so that a cadence that alternates between the two does not alternate the gain. `mouse-x` is in logical pixels, so the thresholds are in logical pixels per second and do not change with the display's scale factor, and the preview width the fine gain divides by is in the same unit.
- **Path dependence.** A drag out and back along the same path at different speeds does not return the globe to where it started. Decision 15 accepts this; if it reads as drift in use, the remedy is a narrower blend, not a different rule.
- **Two goldens from the first amendment move on purpose.** `sunrise_band` and `sun_rising_through_the_band` were framed at 60 degrees of sky where the frames nearly agree, so they will move by a little rather than a lot; the record says by how much.
- **The lift may still read as a gap.** Criterion 2 is the judgment and names the remedy.

## Questions, settled

The user's answers of 2026-08-29: the sliders' resolution stays as it is; the precision is wanted in the mouse drag and need not be reflected in the slider; a fine mode behind Shift, which this amendment first proposed, is not wanted, since a hidden modifier is something a user has to know about, and the drag should instead interpret the cursor's acceleration, which is more intuitive; time playback is wanted, but later, on the roadmap; sunrise and sunset are to work correctly at every zoom. The thresholds, the time constant and the fixed angular fine rate are this amendment's proposals for the user to feel out when the implementation can be tried.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the lobe in the true scattering angle and move the Sun's image to the true geometry instead.** That is the mixed-lens question phase B's decision 2 already answered the other way, and reopening it moves every Sun reference and the whole occlusion model. Not taken.
- **A fine mode behind Shift.** This amendment's first proposal, and the user declined it: a modifier is a hidden thing to know, where speed asks nothing of the hand it does not already do. Not taken.
- **A surface-locked base gain**, one screen pixel of painted surface per mouse pixel, replacing the `0.3 × distance / 8` constant. It is the natural rule and would halve today's gearing, but it changes the feel of every drag the user did not complain about, and it does not help the sunrise, whose scale is the sky lens's and not the surface's. Not taken; the constant stays as the coarse gain.
- **A fine gain that divides the coarse gain** by ten or twenty at low speed. Simpler to state, but the coarse gain scales with distance and the sunrise does not, so it would be fine at 3.7 and coarse at 40. The fixed angular gain follows the thing being watched.
- **Blending by the per-event delta rather than by speed.** One line shorter, and wrong on any cadence change: a mouse reporting at 1000 Hz hands over one-pixel deltas at every speed. Speed is delta over time, which is what a hand controls.
- **Scrubbing time instead of turning the globe.** The right long-term answer and the roadmap item; the Date / Time group has an hour slider only, so it would need a new control and is a feature of its own.
